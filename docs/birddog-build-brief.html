What the notes describe

Two agents, not one product

The legend in your notes — □ agent, ○ business, △ group — describes two separate machines that happen to share plumbing. Worth keeping them separate in your head, because only one of them is sellable this month.

  • The Poster (□ → △). Scheduled posting into neighborhood groups on behalf of a business, with the business approving copy and creative before anything goes out.
  • The Watcher (□ sits on △). Sits on group feeds, reloads, scans for keywords, and texts the business when someone asks for their trade. This is your speed-to-lead product wearing a different hat.

ICP for both: home services, restaurants, tattoo and barber shops, nonprofits. The Watcher is much stronger for home services specifically, because "anyone know a good roofer in Scarsdale?" is a question people actually type into neighborhood groups, and a plate of food is not.

Sequencing

Lead with the Watcher. It demos in one text message, it shows ROI the first week, and it needs no credentials from the customer. The Poster requires the business's login on day one, which is the hardest ask in the whole product and the riskiest thing you can hold. Sell the Watcher, earn the trust, then offer posting.

Naming

The name, and the constraint behind it

A "bird dog" is already trade vernacular for the person who finds you work and brings it to you. Roofers, remodelers and car dealers use the term unprompted, so it needs no explanation to this ICP — and it's a literal description of the Watcher: sits, watches, flushes out the lead, retrieves it.

BirdDog Recommended

Trade-native, needs no explanation, describes the mechanism exactly. birddog.com and birddog.tv are gone to a video hardware brand — take getbirddog.com or birddogleads.com and register as BirdDog Leads.

First Knock

Pure speed positioning — be the first one at their door. Cleaner story for a cold text; weaker once the Poster joins the lineup.

Territory

Sells the exclusivity — one contractor per town per trade — which is the actual moat. Says nothing about leads, so the tagline has to carry the whole load.

Rules the name has to survive

  • No Facebook, FB, Meta, Group or Book in it. Trademark exposure aside, don't name the company after the platform you're scraping — and it becomes a liability the day you add Nextdoor, Craigslist and town boards.
  • No "AI" or "Agent." This ICP discounts it, and it advertises automation to the platform you'd rather stay boring to.
  • Own the .com. A .io or .ai reads as a scam to a contractor already primed to expect one.
  • Name the outcome, not the mechanism. The mechanism is the part most likely to change under you.
This settles the name now, not after the pilot

SMS is the product, so you need A2P 10DLC registration with the carriers through Twilio. That vetting checks that your brand name, your registered business entity and your website domain all match. Pick the name, register the LLC, buy the matching .com, put the site up — then file the 10DLC campaign. Renaming after registration means re-vetting, and unregistered traffic gets filtered or dropped silently, which kills the entire product without an error message.

Whatever the company ends up called, keep the internal names: the Watcher and the Poster. They're clear on an ops board, and they let you sell monitoring standalone without the brand implying you also auto-post.

Agent 2 · Build sequence

The Watcher

Goal: under 90 seconds from someone posting "need a plumber in Rye" to that plumber's phone buzzing. Everything below serves that number.

  1. Define the territory grid

    Town × trade is the unit of inventory and the unit of sale. Seed it with towns you can actually get into, not the whole county.

  2. Get into the groups — start now

    A warmed, human-looking account requesting to join each neighborhood group. Admin approval takes days to weeks and some never approve. This is the long pole of the entire business; every week you delay here is a week of dead inventory.

  3. Write the keyword list per trade

    Not just the noun. The ask-phrasings and the symptoms: "roofer", "roof leak", "shingles", "anyone know a good", "recommendations for", plus common misspellings. People describe the problem far more often than the trade.

  4. Build the poll loop

    Persistent browser session, load each group's feed sorted by recent, on an interval with jitter so the cadence isn't machine-regular. Dedupe on post ID so one post never fires twice.

  5. Classify before alerting

    An LLM pass on each new post: is this real hire intent, for this trade, in this town? This is what separates a product from a keyword firehose — it kills "anyone have a recommendation for a good preschool" before it reaches the customer's phone.

  6. Fire the SMS

    What was asked, which group, who asked, a direct link, and a suggested reply they can paste. The suggested reply is what turns a notification into a closed job.

  7. Quiet hours and a daily cap

    No 2am texts, and a ceiling on alerts per day. One irrelevant 11pm buzz costs you more trust than three good leads earn.

  8. The business replies — not the agent

    They comment in the group themselves, in their own voice, as themselves. Auto-replying is the single fastest way to get an account killed and the customer's name attached to spam. Hold this line even when customers ask you to cross it.

  9. Log every outcome

    Alert sent, replied or not, job won or not. At the end of the free week this log is your close: "we sent you eleven, you replied to four, you booked two."

Agent 1 · Build sequence

The Poster

Your notes already contain the important architectural instinct here: the agent that holds the credentials is not the agent that posts.

  1. Credential handoff — the hard ask

    The business's login. Prefer a Page role invite or a delegated access path over holding a password wherever the platform allows it; a stored customer password is a liability you carry indefinitely, including after they churn.

  2. Spawn the posting-only agent (□²)

    The separation of duties from your notes. The credential-holder never posts; a second, narrowly scoped agent does nothing but publish already-approved copy. If the poster misbehaves or is compromised, the blast radius is one post, not one account.

  3. Generate copy and creative into a queue

    Drafted per business, per group, held for review. Nothing reaches a group unreviewed.

  4. Business approves — hard gate

    Explicit approval of both copy and creative before anything publishes. This was underlined in your notes and it should stay non-negotiable: it's your liability shield and it's the reason a business will trust you with the account at all.

  5. Schedule Mon / Wed / Thu

    Per-group cadence caps and staggered times. The same post hitting six groups within a minute is the signature that gets accounts flagged.

  6. Check each group's own rules

    Promo rules differ per group — some ban business posts outright, some allow one day a week. Maintain a per-group allow/deny list or you'll get the customer banned from their own neighborhood.

  7. Publish and capture the permalink

    Store the live URL for the report, and so the business can go engage with replies themselves.

  8. Decide the comment question

    Your margin note flagged boosting comments as possibly unwanted. Recommendation: don't. Auto-commenting to boost reach is the highest-risk, lowest-trust behavior in the whole system.

Signup

Three slot states, one gate

Town and trade come first on the form, before name or email, because that's what determines whether they can sign up at all. The moment they pick, the server answers one of three ways.

StateMeansWhat they see
Open You watch that town, slot is free "Scarsdale roofing is open. Claim it." Straight through to details.
Taken Someone holds that town × trade "Scarsdale roofing is claimed." Offer the waitlist or a nearby town. This is the exclusivity promise doing its job in public.
Uncovered You're not in that town's groups yet "We're not live in Rye yet — reserve your slot, live in about two weeks." Loud, on the payment screen, not in fine print.

Then: name, business, cell, email — and verify the cell with a one-time code right there. The entire product is SMS, so a wrong or fake number means they get nothing and churn on day one. Catch it at signup.

Then a light contractor check. Because a slot is exclusive, you can't let a tire-kicker — or worse, a competitor blocking a rival — lock Scarsdale roofing. Business name, a website or business page, confirm they do that trade in that area. You eyeball it yourself early; automate it later.

Card up front? Not for the pilot

Contractors are card-shy, the free week is the whole pitch, and you're closing by text or call at the end anyway. Collecting a card adds friction for no benefit while onboarding is manual. Flip to card-on-file with a Stripe trial once you're automated and the human close stops scaling.

Architecture

The page is the storefront, not the store

The Vercel page handles marketing, the slot board, the form and checkout handoff. What it cannot do alone: know that Scarsdale roofing is taken, verify a phone, charge a card safely, or turn the Watcher on. Anything involving money, real availability or account access runs server-side where a visitor can't tamper with it — if availability lived in the page's code, two people could claim the same slot and you'd never know.

Vercel pageMarketing, slot board, form. Asks the server; never decides.
→
API + databaseThe truth: slots, claims, verification, customers.
→
Stripe CheckoutHosted, so card details never touch your code.
→
Activation queueCovered town: automatic. Uncovered: your ops list.

Vercel can host both halves — the marketing front end and serverless API routes against a Postgres database — so this isn't a second stack, but the server layer has to exist.

Don't build this first

For the pilot, the form can just email you the signup. You check the slot by hand, verify and onboard by text, no Stripe, no availability API. That's a legitimate signup for the first three to five customers and it launches this week instead of after the backend. Build the machine once you've proven by hand that people want to get on it — and once the scraper has proven durable enough to be worth automating around.

Open items

What could break this

  • Platform automation rules. Both agents operate against Facebook's automation policy. Account durability is an operational cost, not an edge case — budget for accounts dying and plan how a customer's coverage survives it.
  • Credential custody. Holding a customer's login for the Poster is a real liability. Decide your storage, rotation and offboarding story before the first customer hands one over, not after.
  • Carrier registration. 10DLC brand vetting gates the entire SMS channel and takes time. It's on the critical path with group approvals, so start both now.
  • Exclusivity integrity. The moment you sell a second roofer in the same town, the promise is dead and the story travels. The slot table has to be the single source of truth, server-side, with no manual overrides.
  • Honest wait times. Taking money for an uncovered town and going quiet for two weeks is the fastest way to torch the brand with an audience already expecting a scam.
